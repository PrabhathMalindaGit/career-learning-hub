Community Solar Pilot Brief
Page 1 - Project overview

The fictional Greenbridge Community Solar Pilot is a 12-month programme designed to test shared solar access for apartment residents who cannot install panels on their own roofs.
The pilot will recruit 120 households. Each participating household receives a monthly bill credit based on its allocated share of electricity generated by the community array.
The pilot's primary goals are to measure household participation, estimate bill-credit value, and record operational issues before any larger rollout.

Community Solar Pilot Brief
Page 2 - Eligibility and budget

Households are eligible if they live within the Greenbridge municipal boundary, are current electricity customers, and do not already receive credit from another shared-solar programme.
The pilot budget is 180,000 fictional currency units. Of this amount, 110,000 is reserved for equipment and installation, 40,000 for operations and maintenance, and 30,000 for participant support and evaluation.
Participants do not pay an enrolment fee during the pilot.

Community Solar Pilot Brief
Page 3 - Timeline and monitoring

Months 1 to 2 cover recruitment and account setup. Months 3 to 4 cover installation and system commissioning. Electricity generation and bill-credit monitoring run from Month 5 through Month 12.
The project team will record monthly generation, aggregate participant bill credits, support requests, and system downtime.
A mid-pilot review is scheduled at the end of Month 8.

Community Solar Pilot Brief
Page 4 - Risks and limitations

The pilot does not guarantee a fixed bill saving because solar generation varies with weather and equipment availability.
The evaluation is limited to 120 households in one municipality, so results should not automatically be generalized to all households or regions.
If the array is unavailable for more than 48 consecutive hours, the project team must log the incident and notify participants through the study communication channel.
